About Me

Feel free to read a little more about my background.

Back in school, I always enjoyed maths and computer science. A lot of that started with playing computer games when I was younger, which was also my first reason for wanting to learn how to code (eventually being able to program my own game, which I think is a dream shared by many when they’re still teenagers). When I was 15, I spent six months in Silicon Valley with my dad, living in Mountain View and attending the German International School of Silicon Valley. Some of the people I got to know there worked at tech companies, including Google, and I also got to visit Google’s campus, which I still remember to this day. I can’t say exactly what amazed me so much back then, but that experience definitely stayed with me and made working in this field feel a little more tangible.

After graduating from school in 2022 with the plan to go ahead and start uni, I was pretty certain I wanted to go for either cs or maths. I knew Bonn, which is really close to where I grew up, had a very strong maths department, so I figured it must also be pretty good for computer science. Especially for theoretical computer science, that turned out to be very true. As I started taking more specialised courses, I became increasingly interested in algorithms (particularly graph algorithms), which eventually led me to work in that field as a research assistant at Fraunhofer FKIE in late 2023.

There, I started working on the Airlift Challenge, a competition organised by the AFRL, which turned out to be quite a lot of fun. Alongside that, I continued taking related courses at university and eventually wrote my bachelor’s thesis in 2025 on using memetic algorithms to solve dynamic routing problems.

During my bachelor’s, I also took some econ courses on game theory and auctions, which I really enjoyed, and looking back, they had quite a bit in common with what interested me about algorithms, since both are about figuring out how to make good decisions, especially when information is incomplete or other people’s decisions affect the outcome. In my master’s, courses on Algorithms and Uncertainty and Algorithmic Game Theory complemented that nicely as I then got the chance to look more closely at the algorithmic side. This is essentially the closest thing to a common thread through my current interests, which mostly come down to using heuristics or ML to inform decision making in settings with uncertainty, whether that means solving classical optimisation problems, finding better strategies in games or something else.

Besides uni and work, a friend and I also joined a Formula Student racing team in 2024 to try something new (since we already spent enough time on software). We spent most of our time working on the aerodynamics of the car’s rear wing, iterating between CAD designs and CFD simulations, and later also got some hands-on experience manufacturing parts of the vehicle. I really enjoyed working on something that eventually became a physical thing you could see and touch.

After a somewhat stressful thesis period in late summer 2025, I briefly considered moving to Munich to continue my studies at TUM, but eventually decided to stay in Bonn for my master’s. I wanted to continue my work at Fraunhofer while also finding ways to get a little more involved in academic research. Through courses at the university’s CAISA Lab, I got to work on some pretty cool projects under the supervision of PhD students and postdocs, which also gave me the opportunity to co-author a paper on reinforcement learning and emergent misalignment, presented at EMNLP 2026. I really enjoy working with the people there. Most of them are still fairly young themselves, so working on papers together feels engaging and collaborative rather than formal. Along the way, I was also fortunate to receive the Deutschlandstipendium, or Germany Scholarship.

At the moment, I’m an Applied Scientist Intern at DHL Group, working on route optimisation and planning under uncertainty. One question I work on is how many drivers to schedule when the day’s tours are already planned but the day itself is still uncertain, for example because more parcels arrive than expected. Using historical information to make better decisions in situations like this fits really well with what I described earlier. There’s also a small connection back to Mountain View: regularly encountering tools like Google Maps and Street View at work reminds me of that early visit and how research and engineering eventually become part of everyday decisions.

After the internship and a nice time in Madrid, I’m planning to do another internship before deciding whether to start working or pursue a PhD. I’m still figuring out what makes sense, including how to think strategically about a future in which AI might outperform me in almost every domain (which is still hard to grasp but I think eventually inevitable). That’s a slightly strange backdrop to planning a career while arguably still being very young as well, but also something I have limited control over. What I do know is that I enjoy learning, working through difficult problems and finding out how things work. For now, that seems like a good reason to keep going.
